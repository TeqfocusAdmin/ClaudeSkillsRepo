# Reference: Pricing Presentation — Teqfocus

How to present, position, and defend Teqfocus pricing in a deal.
This is not about discounting — it's about framing value before price.

## Pricing Conversation Framework

### Step 1: Establish the cost of the status quo BEFORE presenting price

Calculate (or estimate) what the problem is currently costing:
- Manual hours: "How many hours/week is your team spending on [X]?"
- Missed revenue: "What's the value of deals that slip due to poor pipeline data?"
- Risk exposure: "What does a failed AI implementation cost in time and credibility?"

Frame before price:
"Based on what you've shared, the status quo is costing roughly [estimate].
Our engagement starts at [price]. Let's talk about what changes."

### Step 2: Anchor on value, not just deliverables

Wrong: "For $50K, we will deliver a Data Maturity Assessment including 4 workshops,
a 40-page report, and a roadmap."

Right: "For $50K, you'll have complete clarity on what's blocking your AI roadmap,
a prioritized path to production, and a team that's done this 40+ times for
healthcare organizations just like yours."

### Step 3: Handle the "That's expensive" objection

**Option A — Reframe to ROI:**
"Compared to what? An Accenture team spends 3 months ramping on your platform
before they start. We've been building on Salesforce and Snowflake since 2012.
You're paying for delivered outcomes, not learning time."

**Option B — Break it into phases:**
"Start with the $50K assessment. At the end, you have a roadmap and a decision.
If it's not the right fit, you've still got a $50K asset. If it is — we move."

**Option C — Quantify the alternative:**
"What would it cost to have your internal team do this? 6 months of 3
engineers at [burdened rate] is [X]. And they still wouldn't have the
Salesforce and Data Cloud specialization our team brings."

### Step 4: Never discount without a reason

Discounting without a concession signals the price was inflated to begin with.
If flexibility is needed:
- Reduce scope, not margin: "We can start with phases 1–2 and hold phase 3"
- Accelerate decision: "If you can commit by [date], we can hold current pricing"
- Bundle value: "If you're committing to both phases now, we can structure it as [X]"

## When to Walk Away

Qualify out if:
- Prospect wants to negotiate before even understanding the scope
- Budget is below minimum viable engagement size for Teqfocus
- Decision-maker isn't in the room and won't be
- They've already chosen another vendor and are just validating

Staying in a deal you can't win or won't price fairly wastes AE time
and dilutes Teqfocus's positioning.
