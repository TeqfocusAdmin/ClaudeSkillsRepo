# Objection-Handling Guide — Teqfocus Sales Enablement

## How to Use This Guide
Each objection entry includes: what the prospect actually means (not just what they said), the wrong way to respond, and the right response. Responses are written as talk tracks — ready to use in a conversation.

**Rule:** Never argue. Never get defensive. Acknowledge → reframe → evidence.

---

## Objection Category 1: Price / Budget

### "You're too expensive."
**What they actually mean:** I'm not sure the ROI justifies the cost — or I'm comparing you to a cheaper offshore vendor.

**Wrong response:** "We're actually quite competitive for the market..."

**Right response:**
> "That's fair — and it's a question worth answering directly. The difference usually comes down to what you're comparing. If you're comparing us to an offshore delivery shop, yes, our rates are higher. But the cost of a failed implementation — the rework, the re-architecture, the lost team confidence — tends to dwarf the rate difference. We've taken over projects from lower-cost vendors multiple times. Happy to walk through what that cleanup typically costs versus what a right-first-time engagement looks like."

---

### "We don't have budget right now."
**What they actually mean:** This isn't a priority, or I need to build the business case.

**Wrong response:** "We can work with almost any budget..."

**Right response:**
> "Totally understand. A few questions: Is there a quarter where this gets revisited, or is it more of a reprioritization situation? And is the problem we've been discussing something that's costing you in the meantime — or is it more of a future-state investment? The reason I ask is that some of what we do can start as a scoped assessment — lower cost, fast value — which often helps teams make the internal case."

## Objection Category 2: Competitor / Alternative

### "We're already working with [Salesforce-only SI / big firm]."
**What they actually mean:** Why would I switch? / What's wrong with what I have?

**Wrong response:** "They're fine, but we're better because..."

**Right response:**
> "Makes sense — they do good Salesforce work. The question I'd ask is: what's on your roadmap beyond the CRM layer? If AI, data modernization, or Snowflake are in the picture, that's where the single-layer model starts to break down — you end up coordinating between your Salesforce partner and a separate data vendor, and nobody owns the connection. That gap is where we typically come in. If that's not a gap you're experiencing, no problem. But if it is, worth a conversation."

---

### "We're considering [offshore vendor] — they're significantly cheaper."
**Right response:**
> "We hear that a lot, and the rate difference is real. What's also real is what we see when those engagements run into trouble — and they often do on complex Salesforce + data work, because it requires Summit-level depth that's hard to replicate offshore. We've stepped into several of those situations. The question isn't really rates vs. rates — it's: what does a delayed or redone implementation cost you? We can work through that math together if it's useful."

---

### "We're going to evaluate a few partners before deciding."
**Right response:**
> "That's exactly the right move — and we'd expect that. What I'd suggest is making sure the evaluation criteria include the full stack: not just Salesforce depth, but what happens when your data layer and your AI roadmap need to connect to it. A lot of evaluations focus on CRM credentials alone, and then discover the data gap later in the engagement. Happy to put together a structured comparison framework if that would help your team."

## Objection Category 3: Timing

### "This isn't a priority right now."
**Right response:**
> "Understood. Is it not a priority because it's not urgent, or because there are other things ahead of it on the list? The reason I ask is that some of the problems we're talking about — like AI stalling at the data layer, or Salesforce adoption sitting at 40% — compound the longer they sit. Not saying that to create pressure, but it's worth knowing what the cost of delay looks like in your context. Happy to leave you with a one-pager on how similar teams have framed the urgency for their leadership."

---

### "We just finished a big implementation — the team needs a break."
**Right response:**
> "That makes complete sense, and we've been in that position with clients. What I'd offer is this: a lightweight architecture or roadmap review — not an implementation, just a structured look at where the gaps are and what's worth prioritizing next. Takes 3–4 weeks, minimal team lift, and gives you something concrete to bring to leadership when the timing is right. Would that kind of scoped engagement be useful?"

## Objection Category 4: Trust / Risk

### "We've had bad experiences with SIs before."
**What they actually mean:** I'm worried you'll overpromise and underdeliver.

**Right response:**
> "I appreciate you saying that — it's probably the most honest thing a prospect can tell us. The specific things that tend to cause those experiences are: ramp-up time billed to the client, handoffs between delivery teams, and deliverables without adoption. Here's how we work differently: [specific delivery model point — phased approach, named accountable lead, defined success metrics from Day 1]. I'd also point you to [specific reference or case study] — the outcome matters, but so does how the engagement ran."

---

### "You're smaller than [big firm] — can you handle our scale?"
**Right response:**
> "It's a fair question. What I'd reframe is: what does scale actually require in this context? If it requires 200 generalist consultants, that's one thing. If it requires deep Salesforce Summit expertise, a Snowflake-certified data architecture team, and an AI practice that's done this in your industry — that's what we bring, and we've done it at enterprise scale. The clients who've had complexity challenges with us have been the exception, not the rule — happy to connect you with a reference in your industry if that would help."

## Objection Category 5: Product / Capability

### "We're not sure you know our industry well enough."
**Right response:**
> "That's exactly the right bar to hold us to — and it's the question we'd ask of any partner. Here's what I'd offer: [specific industry proof point — client type, problem solved, outcome]. Our [industry] practice has been running for [X years / X clients] — and we have dedicated practitioners who have lived these problems, not generalists who've read about them. Let me put you in touch with [name / reference type] who can speak to that directly."

---

### "We already have Salesforce internally — we don't need a partner."
**Right response:**
> "Good starting point. The question we'd ask is: what's on the roadmap that your internal team doesn't have bandwidth for? Most in-house Salesforce teams are deep on BAU — support tickets, releases, admin work — and the strategic work (AI, Data Cloud, architecture decisions) gets deprioritized. We're not here to replace your internal team. We're here to do the work they can't get to, and leave them with something they can own and maintain."

## Handling Objections: Universal Rules

1. **Never argue or get defensive** — it puts the prospect in a corner
2. **Acknowledge first** — "That's a fair point" or "I understand why you'd think that"
3. **Ask a clarifying question before responding** — make sure you're addressing the real concern
4. **Use proof, not claims** — a client story beats a positioning statement every time
5. **End with a question or a low-friction offer** — keep the conversation moving
6. **Flag unverified metrics** — never make up numbers to win an argument. It destroys trust when discovered.
