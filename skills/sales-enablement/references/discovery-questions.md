# Discovery Question Framework — Teqfocus Sales Enablement

## Purpose
Discovery questions do two things simultaneously: they help the rep understand the prospect's real situation, and they help the prospect surface problems they may not have fully articulated. Good discovery makes the sales conversation feel like a consultation — not an interrogation.

**Rule:** Never ask a discovery question you're not prepared to respond to intelligently.

---

## Discovery Framework: The Four Zones

### Zone 1 — Current State (Where Are They Now)
Understand what they have, what's working, and what isn't — before diagnosing anything.

**Data & AI Questions:**
- "How would you describe your current data architecture — is it centralized, distributed, or still largely siloed by department?"
- "When your data and analytics teams need to move quickly, what slows them down most?"
- "Where is your AI initiative today — are you pre-POC, post-POC, or in production?"
- "What does your current Snowflake / Databricks / cloud data setup look like — or is that still being evaluated?"

**Salesforce / CRM Questions:**
- "How would you rate your current Salesforce adoption — do your reps actually live in it, or is it more of a reporting layer?"
- "What percentage of your CRM data would you say is clean and trusted by your sales leadership?"
- "When your team needs something done in Salesforce, how long does it typically take to get it built or changed?"
- "What's living outside of Salesforce today that probably shouldn't be — spreadsheets, other tools, manual processes?"

**Architecture / IT Questions:**
- "Who owns the connection between your data infrastructure and your Salesforce environment — is that one team, or does it span multiple?"
- "When your last major technology project stalled, where in the stack did it happen?"
- "How many vendors are currently involved in your data + CRM + AI stack — and who coordinates between them?"

---

### Zone 2 — Pain & Consequence (What's It Costing Them)
Move from describing the situation to understanding business impact. This is where deals accelerate.

**Impact Questions:**
- "When [specific problem they've described] happens — what does that actually cost you? Is that measurable?"
- "How does this affect [specific persona — sales team, clinical staff, finance team] on a week-to-week basis?"
- "Has this come up at the leadership level — is there pressure to fix it, or is it more of a known issue people have learned to work around?"
- "When AI stalls or Salesforce underperforms, where does the blame typically land internally?"
- "If this problem stays unresolved for another 12 months — what does that look like for your team?"

**Urgency Questions:**
- "Is there a hard deadline attached to this — a contract renewal, a board commitment, a regulatory requirement?"
- "What's driving the timing for looking at this now versus six months ago?"
- "Who else inside the organization is feeling the impact of this — and are they involved in this conversation?"

---

### Zone 3 — Future State (What Does Good Look Like)
Help them articulate the outcome they want — in their words. This becomes the basis for your proposal.

- "If we solved this completely — what does your environment look like 18 months from now?"
- "What would it mean for your team if Salesforce was actually being used at 80%+ adoption — what changes?"
- "When you imagine your AI initiative working the way it's supposed to — what decisions is it powering that you can't make today?"
- "What does a successful partnership with an SI look like to you — based on your past experiences, good and bad?"
- "How will you measure whether this engagement was worth it?"

---

### Zone 4 — Decision Process (How Do They Buy)
Understand the buying process before you go deep. Avoid proposal-writing for deals that aren't real.

- "Who else needs to be involved in a decision like this — is it primarily you, or does it go broader?"
- "When you've made decisions like this in the past, what's the typical process — evaluation, committee, procurement?"
- "Is there a budget allocated for this, or would this require a budget conversation internally?"
- "What would need to be true for you to feel confident moving forward — proof of concept, references, something else?"
- "Are you evaluating other partners alongside us — and if so, what criteria are you using?"
- "What's your timeline — is there a target date for a decision or a go-live?"

## Industry-Specific Discovery Angles

### Healthcare
- "How many EMR or source systems are you currently pulling patient data from?"
- "Where does your care coordination break down — is it data availability, workflow, or both?"
- "How are you currently addressing SDOH factors in your risk stratification models?"
- "Is interoperability on your roadmap — and where does it sit in terms of priority versus compliance pressure?"

### Telecom
- "Where is churn showing up most — consumer, SMB, or enterprise — and how predictive is your current model?"
- "What's your current field service completion rate, and how much of your manual dispatch is still manual?"
- "How connected is your CRM to your network operations data right now?"

### Financial Services
- "How much time do your advisors spend on data gathering versus actual client conversation?"
- "Where do compliance requirements create the most friction in your current workflow?"
- "How unified is your client 360 today — can a relationship manager see the full picture in one place?"

### Hi-Tech / SaaS
- "Where does your PLG motion hand off to your sales team — and how smooth is that transition in Salesforce?"
- "What's your current CS-to-expansion conversion rate, and how much of that is tracked in your CRM?"
- "Where does your CPQ process break down — configuration complexity, approval routing, or something else?"

## Discovery Rules

1. **Ask one question at a time** — stacked questions let the prospect answer the easiest one and skip the rest
2. **Silence is productive** — after a good question, let the silence work. Don't fill it.
3. **Dig when something surfaces** — "Tell me more about that" is the most powerful follow-up
4. **Take notes visibly** — signals that their answer matters
5. **Reflect back what you heard** before moving on — it builds trust and ensures accuracy
6. **Never diagnose before you've completed Zone 1 and Zone 2** — premature solutions destroy credibility
7. **Zone 4 questions earn their place** — don't jump to decision process before you've established real pain
