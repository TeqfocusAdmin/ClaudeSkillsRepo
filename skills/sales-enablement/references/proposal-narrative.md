# Proposal / SOW Narrative Reference — Teqfocus Sales Enablement

## Purpose
The proposal narrative is the written document that accompanies a Statement of Work or formal engagement proposal. Its job is to make the economic buyer feel confident — that Teqfocus understood their problem, designed the right solution, and can be trusted to deliver it.

**Rule:** A proposal that reads like a template loses to one that reads like it was written for this client.

---

## Proposal Narrative Structure

### Section 1: Executive Summary (1 page max)
Written for the economic buyer who won't read the rest.

**Must answer in this order:**
1. What problem are we solving — in the client's language, not Teqfocus' language
2. What Teqfocus is recommending — 2–3 sentences, outcome-led
3. What the client will have at the end — not a deliverable list, a business state
4. Why Teqfocus — 2–3 sentences on the specific reason Teqfocus is right for this engagement

**What NOT to include:**
- Company history or founding story
- Platform feature descriptions
- Anything that could apply to any client

---

### Section 2: Understanding of the Situation (1–2 pages)
Demonstrate that Teqfocus truly heard the discovery conversation.

**Structure:**
- **Current State** — Where the client is today. Specific problems, not general industry trends.
- **Business Impact** — What this costs them: in revenue, time, risk, or opportunity.
- **Why Now** — The trigger that makes this the right time to act.

**Writing rule:** Every sentence in this section should be something the client told you — not something Teqfocus assumed. If you can't anchor a statement to discovery, don't include it.

---

### Section 3: Recommended Approach (2–3 pages)
What Teqfocus is proposing to build, and why this approach.

**Structure:**
- **Guiding Principles** — 3–4 architecture or delivery principles that shaped this recommendation
  - Example: *"Data layer before AI layer — we build the foundation before the model"*
  - Example: *"Adoption-first design — every workflow is built around how your team actually works"*
- **Phase Breakdown** — Each phase with: goal, activities, deliverables, timeline, success criteria
- **Architecture Overview** — What gets built, what integrates with what, what the end state looks like
  - `[DESIGN FLAG: architecture diagram — source systems → data layer → AI/analytics → Salesforce/application]`
- **What Teqfocus Owns vs. What the Client Owns** — Clarity on responsibilities reduces risk perception

---

### Section 4: Why This Works — Proof (1 page)
One or two client stories that are directly relevant to this engagement.

**Format per story:**
- Client context (anonymized if needed): industry, company type, challenge
- What Teqfocus built — 2–3 bullets
- The outcome — metric + timeframe
- The parallel to this engagement — 1 sentence connecting their situation to this one

`[ADD APPROVED CASE STUDY if available — do not fabricate]`

---

### Section 5: Why Teqfocus (Half page)
3–4 reasons specific to this engagement — not generic credential boilerplate.

**Must include:**
- Salesforce Summit Partner — framed as risk reduction for this specific engagement
- Full-stack depth relevant to this project (name only what's relevant)
- Industry-specific experience: client type, problems solved, outcomes
- One human element: the named team or lead who will own this

**Avoid:**
- Listing all certifications
- Describing services not relevant to this engagement
- Anything that reads like a capabilities brochure

---

### Section 6: Investment & Engagement Model (1 page)
Pricing, timeline, and how the engagement is structured.

**Structure:**
- Pricing table: Phase / Scope / Timeline / Investment
- Payment terms (if applicable)
- What's included vs. what's out of scope
- What success looks like at each phase milestone

**Framing note:** Always frame investment in context of the business impact quantified in Section 2. If they told you the problem costs $X annually, the investment should look small by comparison.

---

### Section 7: Next Step (Half page or less)
One clear, specific action.

✅ *"To proceed, we'd recommend a 2-hour kickoff workshop with [names / roles] to finalize the scope and agree on success criteria for Phase 1. [Name] from Teqfocus will reach out by [date] to schedule."*
✅ *"If the approach looks right, the next step is a 30-minute call to align on the Phase 1 scope and formally kick off. [Calendar link]"*
❌ *"Please don't hesitate to reach out with any questions."*

---

## Proposal Writing Rules

1. **Write Section 2 first** — if you can't write it from discovery notes, you don't know enough yet
2. **Client language throughout** — use the words the client used in discovery, not Teqfocus jargon
3. **Every phase needs a success criterion** — not just a deliverable list
4. **Metrics anchor the investment** — connect the investment back to the cost of the problem in Section 2
5. **Short paragraphs** — 2–4 lines max. Proposals get skimmed — make every section scannable
6. **Flag unverified numbers** — `[VERIFY]` — never fabricate case study metrics in a proposal
7. **Name the team** — "Teqfocus" is abstract. "[Name], your engagement lead" is human and accountable
8. **Design flags where needed:**
   - `[DESIGN FLAG: architecture diagram]`
   - `[DESIGN FLAG: phase timeline / Gantt visual]`
   - `[DESIGN FLAG: metric callout boxes in proof section]`
   - `[DESIGN FLAG: partner logo strip — Salesforce, Snowflake, etc.]`
