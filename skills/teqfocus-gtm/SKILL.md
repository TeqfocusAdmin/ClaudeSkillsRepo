## Section 2: The Multi-Cloud Positioning Story

This is Teqfocus' **core strategic differentiator**. Use it deliberately — not as a platform list.

### The Narrative
Most enterprise technology problems are **architecture problems**, not platform problems. Data lives in silos. AI stalls because the foundation isn't ready. Salesforce underperforms because it isn't connected to where data actually lives.

Teqfocus solves this by working **across the full stack** — from data infrastructure (Snowflake, Databricks, cloud) through the intelligence layer (AI/ML, analytics) to the application layer (Salesforce, custom apps) — as a **single, integrated partner**.

❌ "Our multi-cloud expertise spans several platforms."

## Section 3: Target Personas

Always confirm the target persona before writing. Each has a different entry point into the Teqfocus story.

### 🎯 CIO / CTO (Enterprise)
- **Fear:** Another transformation that underdelivers, creates debt, leaves a mess
- **Owns:** Platform stability, integration architecture, cost of ownership
- **Entry Point:** Risk reduction + integration credibility + industry proof
- **Stack to Feature:** Summit Partner credential, MuleSoft, cloud architecture

- **Fear:** AI that the business doesn't trust or use; data strategy without adoption
- **Owns:** Data quality, AI governance, platform ROI, analytics across the business
- **Entry Point:** Data maturity + AI readiness + governed pipelines
- **Stack to Feature:** Snowflake, Databricks, Data Cloud, Agentforce
- **Buying Trigger:** Failed POC, Data Cloud mandate, CEO asking "why isn't AI working"

### 🎯 Mid-Market RevOps / Ops Leader
- **Fear:** Salesforce build the sales team ignores; customization that breaks every release
- **Owns:** Pipeline visibility, CRM adoption, sales cycle speed, forecast accuracy
- **Entry Point:** Speed + adoption + practical outcomes in 90 days
- **Stack to Feature:** Sales Cloud, CPQ, Agentforce — light data layer unless there's a gap
- **Buying Trigger:** Salesforce renewal, new VP of Sales, poor adoption, growth breaking process

### 🎯 VP of Sales / Revenue Leader
- **Fear:** Salesforce as an expensive CRM nobody uses; no pipeline visibility
- **Owns:** Revenue target, pipeline health, forecasting accuracy, sales productivity
- **Entry Point:** CRM that actually drives revenue, not just tracks it

## Section 13: First-Attempt Accuracy Rules

Based on usage patterns, these rules prevent the most common output quality issues.

### Before writing ANY asset — confirm these 5 things:
1. **Persona confirmed?** CIO/CTO, CDO, RevOps, VP Sales — never write for "all buyers"
2. **Industry confirmed?** Healthcare, Telecom, FinServ, Retail/CPG, Hi-Tech — never cross-industry without intent
3. **Buyer stage confirmed?** Awareness / Consideration / Decision — asset type depends on this
4. **Named proof point available?** Match case study industry to target. Use `[VERIFY]` if metric unconfirmed.
5. **CTA confirmed?** One specific CTA per asset. Never "contact us to learn more."

### The specificity test — run on every draft:
> "Could this sentence appear in a competitor's asset without changing a word?"
> If YES → rewrite it. If NO → it passes.

### Common output failures to avoid:
- Opening LinkedIn posts with "I" or "We" → always open with an observation, insight, or question
- Subject lines over 50 characters → count before finalizing
- Stacking platform names without outcomes → always connect platform to result
- Announcing Summit Partnership → surface it naturally, once, never as news
- Generic CTAs → always name the meeting type ("30-minute data readiness review", not "let's connect")
- Missing preview text → every email needs a subject + preview text + body + CTA
- Inventing metrics → flag every unconfirmed number with `[VERIFY]`
- Writing for "enterprise and mid-market" simultaneously → pick one scale per asset

### LinkedIn character limits (check before delivering):
- Connection request: 300 characters max → write to 200–250
- DM / InMail body: 1,900 characters max → write to 300–600
- Feed post: 3,000 characters → write to 800–1,300 for best engagement
- Article title: 100 characters → write to 60–80

### Email subject line rules (apply every time):
- Under 50 characters — always count
- No exclamation points
- No "Quick question" (overused)
- No "I wanted to reach out"
- Must be specific to the industry, persona, or company — never generic

### Background rule for every designed asset (slides, PDFs, one-pagers, social cards):
- **Page/slide background: White `#FFFFFF` always — no exceptions**
- Color lives in: headlines (`#59267E`), subheads (`#7751E2`), accent bars, borders, icon fills, CTA buttons, thin top/bottom strips
- The brand gradient (`#BD72EF` → `#8D9BF5`) is used as a thin accent element — never as a full-page fill
- Dark purple (`#59267E`) and Slate Blue (`#7751E2`) appear as text and accent colors — never as page backgrounds
- Footer bars may use `#59267E` — this is the only full-width dark element allowed
- Social quote cards and reel thumbnails: white background with purple text — not inverted
- If a design instruction says "dark background" — override it. White background, apply the brand palette as text/accents on top.

**Reference: Teqfocus SF Practice deck (48 slides) — every slide uses white background with purple/blue content on top. This is the visual standard for all GTM assets.**

## Section 15: Message Testing Framework

Before scaling any campaign message, test it:

**The 5-second test:** Show the headline to a target buyer for 5 seconds.
Ask: "What does this company do? Who is it for? Why does it matter?"
If they can't answer all three — rewrite.

**The "so what?" test:** After every claim, ask "so what?"
"We have 400+ Salesforce certifications" → so what?
→ "You're working with a team that's done this hundreds of times, not learning on your dime"

**The competitor test:** "Could this appear on Accenture or Slalom's website?"
If yes — rewrite with more specificity.

**The buyer language test:** Would a CDO or CIO say this phrase in a meeting?
"Holistic digital transformation" — no. "Getting AI to production" — yes.
