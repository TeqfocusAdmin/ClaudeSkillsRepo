# Reference: Employee Advocacy Posts

For: AEs · Practice leads · Delivery managers · Anyone on the Teqfocus team
sharing content on their personal LinkedIn profile.

Employee advocacy extends Teqfocus reach through authentic voices —
not company page reposts.

## What Makes a Good Advocacy Post

- Personal perspective — "Here's what I've noticed working with healthcare CIOs..."
- Specific insight — not "AI is important" but what the person actually sees at clients
- One idea clearly stated — not a list of everything
- No company promotion — share a perspective, not a pitch

## Post Templates by Role

### AE / Account Executive
Focus: industry trends, deal patterns, what buyers are actually asking

```
[Opening observation from a recent conversation or deal]

[What that tells you about the market or buyer mindset right now]

[1 counterintuitive insight or thing most people miss]

[Optional: what Teqfocus does about it — 1 sentence, natural, not promotional]

[Closing question or invitation to comment]
```

### Practice Lead / Technical
Focus: architecture decisions, lessons from delivery, technical observations

```
[Specific technical challenge encountered recently]

[What the conventional approach gets wrong]

[What actually works and why]

[1 concrete example (anonymized)]

[Takeaway for anyone facing the same situation]
```

### Delivery / Project Manager
Focus: project patterns, change management, client outcomes

```
[Observation from a recent delivery or client situation]

[The real challenge underneath the surface problem]

[What made the difference — specific, not generic]

[What other teams can learn from it]
```

## Rules for Advocacy Posts
- Write in first person — always
- Never say: "I'm excited to share" / "Proud to announce" / "Check out this great content"
- Never repost company content with just "Great read!" — add your own perspective
- Keep to 800–1,200 characters for best engagement
- One hashtag max — optional
- No stock photo suggestions — real posts don't need them
- American English throughout

## What to Avoid
- Don't sound like a press release
- Don't repeat the company page post
- Don't over-promote Teqfocus — authentic advocacy is subtle
- Don't use jargon the person wouldn't actually say in a meeting
