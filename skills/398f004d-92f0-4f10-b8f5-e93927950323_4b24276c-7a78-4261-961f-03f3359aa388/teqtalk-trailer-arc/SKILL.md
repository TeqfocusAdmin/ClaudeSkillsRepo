# Skill: teqtalk-trailer-arc
## Stage 3 — Trailer + Transcript | Cinematic Trailer Script

Read: `framework/BRAND_VOICE.md` · `framework/AUDIENCE_INTEL.md` · `framework/TITLE_FORMULA.md`

---

## What This Skill Produces

The 90-second trailer that drops 24–48 hours before the full episode. It is crafted like a movie trailer — a complete narrative arc compressed into 90 seconds. It opens on the most provocative moment. It builds to a surprising insight. It leaves the central question open. The viewer ends the trailer needing the answer.

**Why this is the highest-leverage skill:**
- Diary of a CEO trailers regularly hit 1M+ views before the full episode
- Trailer views pre-seed the algorithm for the full episode launch
- The trailer is the most-shared asset from any episode
- A strong trailer with 10K–50K pre-release views guarantees the algorithm tests the full episode with a warm audience

---

## The Trailer Architecture

### The 5-Beat Structure (mandatory — no exceptions)

**Beat 1: The Provocation (0–10 seconds)**
Open on the single most provocative, counterintuitive, or high-stakes line from the entire episode. No intro. No "Hi, I'm Jas." No "Today on TeqTalk." The first word is the episode's sharpest edge.

*What to look for in the transcript:*
- A statement the guest made that contradicts conventional wisdom
- A number that is bigger or worse than expected
- A confession of a failure that cost real money or career risk
- A prediction that will age badly if they're wrong

**Beat 2: The Stakes (10–30 seconds)**
What is at risk? What decision is being debated? Name the consequence of getting this wrong. The viewer must feel: "This is relevant to my situation."

*What to look for:*
- The organisational or career stakes the guest names
- The systemic problem that hasn't been solved
- The thing that surprises the audience about how hard this actually is

**Beat 3: The Surprising Insight (30–55 seconds)**
One counterintuitive truth from the episode. Not a summary — a reveal. Something the viewer didn't know before and now can't unknow.

*What to look for:*
- The moment Jas says "Wait — that's not what I expected"
- The data point that inverts the conventional narrative
- The behind-the-scenes reality that contradicts the public story

**Beat 4: The Open Question (55–75 seconds)**
Do NOT resolve the tension. Leave the most important question in the episode unanswered. The viewer must click to find the answer.

*What to look for:*
- The decision the guest is still wrestling with
- The question that the episode answers but the trailer doesn't
- The challenge that the audience will recognise from their own experience

**Beat 5: The CTA (75–90 seconds)**
One line. One action. "Full episode out [date/now]. Link in bio."

## The 4-Iteration Loop

### Pass 1 — Raw Draft
Mine the transcript for the 5 beats. Write the first complete trailer script. Use near-exact dialogue from the transcript for Beats 1, 3, and 4. Write connective tissue for Beats 2 and 5.

### Pass 2 — Opening Sharpness Test
Benchmark the opening 10 seconds against this standard: "Does this opening make a CIO stop scrolling at 11pm?" If the answer is anything other than a clear yes — find a sharper opening. Check the transcript again for a harder, more specific line.

### Pass 3 — Narrative Integrity Check
Read the full 90-second script as a complete story. Does it have tension, build, and an unresolved question? If it feels like a highlight reel rather than a trailer — restructure. A trailer tells a story. A highlight reel shows moments.

### Pass 4 — Platform Adaptation
Create the three platform-specific versions from the approved 90-second master:
- YouTube: 90 seconds, full arc
- LinkedIn: 60 seconds (cut Beat 2 or compress Beat 4)
- Reel: 30 seconds (Beat 1 + Beat 3 + CTA only)

## Quality Test

Read the 90-second script aloud. At the end, ask: "What question am I left with that I need to watch the full episode to answer?" If there is no clear unanswered question — the trailer has resolved too much. Rewrite Beat 4.

The trailer passes when a CXO who watches it feels: "I need to finish this conversation."
