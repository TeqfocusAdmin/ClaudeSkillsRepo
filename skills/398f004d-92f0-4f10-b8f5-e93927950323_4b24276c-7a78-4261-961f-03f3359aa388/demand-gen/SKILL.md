## Section 2: Demand Gen Principles

These govern every asset produced by this skill.

1. **Never pitch in the first touch.** Open with insight, relevance, or a problem — not a product or company introduction.
2. **One CTA per email.** Not three options. One clear next step that is easy to take.
3. **Personalization is the price of entry.** Reference the industry, the role, and a specific trigger where possible. Generic emails get deleted.
4. **Subject line's only job = get the open.** Curiosity or benefit. Under 50 characters. Never "Check out our..." or "Exciting news from Teqfocus."
5. **Proof before pitch.** Teqfocus credentials enter the sequence at Email 3 or later — as evidence, not as an announcement.
6. **MQL conversion = meeting booked.** Every sequence must have a clear path to a calendar link or a defined next step.
7. **Sequences respect timing.** Cold outbound: 3–5 days between emails. Nurture: 5–7 days. Event follow-up: Email 1 within 24 hours.

## Section 3: Buyer Personas & Conversion Triggers

### CIO / CTO (Enterprise)
- **What moves them:** Risk reduction + proven architecture + Summit credential
- **What they fear:** Another failed transformation; integration debt; AI initiative without a foundation
- **Entry point in a sequence:** Industry-specific architecture problem or failed project observation
- **Meeting ask:** "Architecture review" or "30-minute technical assessment"
- **Buying triggers:** Failed project, AI board mandate, M&A integration, cloud migration stall

### CDO / Head of Data & AI
- **What moves them:** Data maturity proof + AI readiness framework + governed pipelines
- **What they fear:** AI the business doesn't trust; data strategy without adoption; failed POC
- **Entry point:** Data layer failure mode or AI ROI gap observation
- **Meeting ask:** "Data readiness review" or "AI readiness workshop"
- **Buying triggers:** Failed AI POC, Data Cloud mandate, CEO asking "why isn't AI working"

### RevOps / Ops Leader (Mid-Market)
- **What moves them:** Speed + CRM adoption + practical 90-day outcomes
- **What they fear:** Salesforce nobody uses; customization that breaks; manual forecasting
- **Entry point:** Specific CRM adoption or forecast accuracy problem
- **Meeting ask:** "CRM health check" or "30-minute Salesforce review"
- **Buying triggers:** Salesforce renewal, new VP of Sales, poor adoption, growth breaking process

### VP of Sales / Revenue Leader
- **What moves them:** Revenue impact, pipeline visibility, forecast accuracy
- **What they fear:** Salesforce as an expensive CRM nobody uses; missed quarters
- **Entry point:** Pipeline visibility or forecast accuracy gap
- **Meeting ask:** "Revenue operations review" or "pipeline visibility session"
- **Buying triggers:** Missed quarters, manual forecasting, new sales motion

## Section 4: Email & Copy Rules

### Always
- Open with an industry observation or a question — never about Teqfocus
- Name the industry and role — generic copy gets deleted
- Use short paragraphs (2–3 lines max)
- One CTA per email — specific and low-friction
- End sequences with a non-pressured "break-up" email
- Flag unverified stats with `[VERIFY]`

### Never
- "I wanted to reach out..."
- "I hope this email finds you well..."
- "As a leader in the industry..."
- "We are excited to share..."
- Stack platform names in the first 2 emails
- Use more than one CTA per email
- Mention Summit credential before Email 3

### Subject Line Rules
- Under 50 characters
- No exclamation points
- No "Quick question" (overused)
- Avoid question-only subjects — combine with a hook
- Best formats: *Observation + Gap*, *Specific Problem*, *Named Outcome*

## Section 8: Sender Tier Rules (Updated)

Every email asset produced by this skill must specify which sender tier it's written for.
Tone, personalization depth, and subject line style differ significantly by tier.

### SDR / BDR (High Volume)
- Tone: Research-personalized but efficient — sounds human, not robotic
- Subject lines: Industry-observation format — not named-account format
- Personalization: Industry + role + one company signal (job posting, news)
- Volume: 50–200 contacts per sequence run
- When to use: Tier 2/3 accounts, first-pass cold outbound, event general invites

### AE / Account Owner (Medium Volume)
- Tone: Account-specific, considered, relationship-aware
- Subject lines: Company-specific or outcome-specific
- Personalization: Deep — references specific company situation from ABM brief
- Volume: 10–50 contacts
- When to use: Tier 1 ABM accounts, warm prospects, post-event follow-up

### Exec / GTM Leader (Low Volume)
- Tone: Executive peer-to-peer — brief, direct, no marketing language
- Subject lines: Ultra-specific — named company, named problem, or named opportunity
- Personalization: The email should feel hand-written for this one person
- Volume: 1–10 contacts
- When to use: CXO targets at Tier 1 accounts, stuck deals, VIP event invites, partner intros

**Always specify the sender tier at the top of any sequence delivered.**

## Section 9: Email Deliverability Rules

Protecting Teqfocus sender reputation in HubSpot outbound:

**Technical:**
- SPF, DKIM, and DMARC configured for teqfocus.com — verify quarterly
- Send from role-specific addresses (avi@teqfocus.com) not info@ or noreply@
- Never buy or scrape email lists — only verified contacts with legitimate interest

**List hygiene:**
- Remove hard bounces immediately after first bounce
- Suppress soft-bounce contacts after 3 consecutive bounces
- Remove unsubscribes instantly — legal requirement
- Suppress contacts with no opens in 6+ months before cold reactivation attempt

**Volume and timing:**
- Never send more than 500 cold emails/day per sender without warming
- Best send times for B2B: Tuesday–Thursday, 8–10am or 1–3pm recipient timezone
- Warm up new domains/inboxes: 50/day week 1, doubling weekly to max volume

**Subject line rules that protect deliverability:**
- Avoid spam trigger words: free, guarantee, winner, limited time, urgent, !!
- Never ALL CAPS in subject lines
- Personalization tokens must have fallback values — broken tokens kill deliverability

## Section 10: Follow-Up Cadence Personalization

Personalize follow-up based on engagement signal, not just time elapsed:

| Signal | Follow-up timing | Message angle |
|---|---|---|
| Opened email, no reply | 3 days | Different angle, same topic |
| Clicked link, no reply | 1–2 days | Reference what they clicked specifically |
| No open | 5–7 days | Different subject line, same audience |
| Replied with question | Same day | Answer the question + advance the conversation |
| Booked a meeting | Confirm + prep email within 1 hour | What to expect, what to prepare |
| Attended event | Within 24 hours | Reference the specific event moment |

**Sequence exit rules:**
- Exit after 5 touches with no engagement (open or click)
- Exit after any reply (positive or negative) — move to 1:1 conversation
- Exit after meeting booked — shift to meeting prep sequence
- Never resume a sequence without reviewing the contact's recent activity first

Every sequence must be precision-targeted. Quality over quantity is not optional — it's structural.

### Comment-to-DM Conversion Funnel
LinkedIn comments from target CIOs/CDOs are warm signals. Systematic approach:

1. Jas or AE comments on a target persona's post with genuine insight (not promotion)
2. If they engage back or like the comment — wait 48 hours
3. Send a connection request with a specific reference to their post
4. After connection — 5-day wait before any message
5. First DM: share a relevant resource (TeqTalk episode or carousel) with zero ask
6. If they engage — 7 days — second DM: specific question about their situation
7. If they respond — book a call

This funnel takes 3–4 weeks but has materially higher conversion than cold InMail.
Track in HubSpot: source = "LinkedIn organic engagement"
