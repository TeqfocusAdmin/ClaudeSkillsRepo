## Section 1: What TeqTalk Is

TeqTalk is Teqfocus' CXO-level podcast series — featuring AI, Data, and Salesforce leaders from enterprises, healthcare systems, financial institutions, and technology companies. Episodes cover real adoption stories, architecture decisions, and executive perspectives — not theory or vendor pitches.

**TeqTalk's GTM role:**
TeqTalk is the most powerful proof point Teqfocus has that it operates at the highest level of the ecosystem. Every episode, every repurposed asset, and every distribution decision should reinforce: *the people shaping enterprise AI, data, and Salesforce want to talk to Teqfocus.*

**What TeqTalk is NOT:**
- A product showcase for Teqfocus services
- A place to pitch Salesforce or Snowflake platforms
- A summary of industry trends the guest has read about
- A generic "let's discuss AI" conversation

**What TeqTalk IS:**
- Real practitioner stories — decisions made, failures experienced, lessons learned
- Architecture-level depth — not surface-level trend commentary
- CXO perspectives on problems that don't have clean answers yet
- The conversation that usually happens in the hallway after a conference, not on the main stage

## Section 2: TeqTalk Guest Profile

### Who the Right Guest Is
- **Title level:** CIO, CTO, CDO, VP of Data/AI, Head of Engineering, Chief Architect, or domain expert with practitioner depth
- **Experience type:** Has built, led, or transformed something — not just studied or advised on it
- **Perspective:** Holds a genuine POV that's specific to their experience — not a recycled industry consensus
- **Industry fit:** Healthcare, Telecom, Financial Services, Retail/CPG, Hi-Tech/SaaS — or cross-industry AI/data/Salesforce practitioner

### What Makes a Guest Compelling
- They've made a decision that most people in their role haven't made yet
- They've failed at something and learned something specific from it
- They're working on a problem that doesn't have a clean solution yet
- Their industry + role combination is one Teqfocus' target audience aspires to or learns from

### What to Avoid in Guest Selection
- Consultants who advise on the work but haven't done it
- Guests who are primarily promoting a book, course, or product
- Guests whose only credential is a large LinkedIn following
- Guests whose perspective is entirely consensus — nothing surprising or challenging

## Section 6: TeqTalk Brand & Voice Rules

### Always
- Guest's insight is the story — Teqfocus is the platform, not the hero
- Extract one sharp idea per asset — never try to summarize the whole episode
- Write reel scripts and social posts in spoken language — not written/formal
- Attribution is always required — never paraphrase a guest without crediting them
- Episode link in every repurposed asset — always give a path back to the full episode

### Never
- "In this episode we discuss..." — lead with the insight, not the format
- Use Teqfocus as the subject of a post built around a guest's insight
- Fabricate or embellish quotes — use exact language or clearly paraphrased with attribution
- Stack multiple insights into one post, reel, or quote card
- Announce Summit Partnership as if it's news in podcast content

### Tone Calibration
Match the guest's energy and language register:
- Technical guest (architect, engineer, data lead) → precise language, specific terminology is welcome
- Storytelling guest (CIO, operator, executive) → narrative tone, concrete examples, less jargon
- Academic or research-led guest (doctor, researcher) → evidence-first framing, credibility signals

### The 1 Episode → 35–45 Pieces Workflow

**Pre-record (week before):**
1. Research brief on guest: public positions, recent quotes, 3 contrarian angles (Claude-assisted)
2. Topic-as-thumbnail pre-test: mock 5–10 thumbnail concepts, run as small LinkedIn/Meta ad ($50–100) to CIO/CDO audience for 3–5 days — winning CTR angle shapes the recording narrative

**Record day:**
3. Riverside or Descript multi-cam capture
4. Flag "hook moments" via Slack timestamps in real time during recording
5. Before guest leaves: re-record 3–5 punchy 10-second lines that didn't quite land — these become cold-open clips

**Post-production (week 0):**
6. Cold-open montage: 2–3 clips, ≤20 seconds, leading with the most contrarian or specific moment
7. Senior editor cuts master episode (60–90 min for TeqTalk). Add YouTube chapters + Spotify chapters. Burn captions into first 60 seconds (LinkedIn requires; 85% of social video is silent)
8. Short-form editor (or Opus Clip / Riverside Magic Clips) cuts 8–12 vertical clips per episode
   - 30–45s for Reels, 45–60s for YouTube Shorts, 60–90s for LinkedIn
9. Designer: 8–15 thumbnail variants (different facial expressions × text hooks)
   - Test top 3 in YouTube's free Test & Compare tool
   - Swap if CTR < 4% after 1,000 impressions in 48-hour window
10. Carousels: 2–3 LinkedIn PDF carousels per episode
    - 8–12 slides, portrait 4:5, one idea per slide, hook on slide 1, save-worthy frameworks slides 2–8, CTA on last

**Distribution (weeks 0–6):**
- Day 0: Full episode on YouTube + Spotify + Apple. Jas personal LinkedIn post with native 60–90s clip + 1,300+ character commentary. TeqTalk newsletter send.
- Days 1–14: 2 clips/day (LinkedIn + YouTube Shorts)
- Day 3: Quote card. Day 7: Carousel.
- Days 15–28: 1 clip/day, evergreen
- Days 29–42: Best-performers reshared with new commentary; episode enters sales enablement pool

### Thumbnail Testing Protocol (Bartlett Method)
- Generate 8–15 thumbnail variants per episode (face expression × text hook × background)
- Pre-launch: run top 3 as LinkedIn/Meta ads at $50–100 budget for 3–5 days — measure CTR
- Post-launch: use YouTube Test & Compare for up to 3 variants

### Episode Clip Hierarchy for LinkedIn
Viral clip signals (human-coded, not AI-detected):
1. Contrarian takes against conventional wisdom
2. Specific numbers or data ("$680K in 4 months")
3. Surprising data the buyer doesn't expect
4. Vulnerable disclosures or honest failure moments
5. "Problem-Agitate-Solution" structure in under 90 seconds

Problem-Agitate-Solution is the dominant B2B viral-clip structure in 2026.
First 3 seconds: specific number or contrarian claim.
Hooks under 2 seconds: 23% higher completion than 4–5 second openings.
