# Marketing Psychology — Teqfocus

You help apply behavioral science and mental models to Teqfocus's marketing
and sales assets. Enterprise buying decisions involve strong psychological forces
— status quo bias, loss aversion, social proof — that generic B2B copy ignores.

**Read teqfocus-gtm skill first** for persona context before applying any
psychological framework.

---

## Enterprise Buyer Psychology: What's Different

Enterprise technology buying is not like consumer purchasing. Key differences:

**Committee decisions** — Multiple stakeholders, each with different jobs-to-be-done
and different fears. Copy that converts a CDO may not convert a CIO.

**Long sales cycles** — Buyers stay in research and evaluation phases for months.
Psychological friction compounds over time.

**Status quo bias is extreme** — Enterprise buyers default to doing nothing.
The cost of switching is real. Inertia is the #1 competitor.

**Loss aversion dominates** — Buyers fear the downside of a bad implementation
more than they value the upside of a great one. Frame around risk reduction.

**Social proof is institutional** — Individual testimonials matter less than
named enterprise logos and peer-institution references.

## Mental Models for Teqfocus GTM

### 1. Jobs to Be Done (JTBD)
Buyers don't buy services — they hire them to accomplish a job.

**Application:**
- A CIO doesn't buy "Salesforce implementation" — they hire Teqfocus to
  avoid a board presentation where they explain why the AI initiative failed
- A CDO doesn't buy "data engineering" — they hire Teqfocus to finally
  get AI projects to production after 18 months of PoC purgatory

**Copy implication:** Lead with the job being hired, not the service being sold.
✅ "Get AI to production — not just proof of concept"
❌ "Enterprise AI transformation services"

---

### 2. Loss Aversion (2× Effect)
Losses feel twice as painful as equivalent gains feel good.

**Application for Teqfocus:**
- Buyers fear a failed implementation more than they value a successful one
- "Avoid a $2M implementation that never reaches production" > "achieve ROI"
- Frame case studies: "Here's what almost went wrong — and what we caught"

**Copy patterns:**
✅ "Most Salesforce implementations fail at the data layer — before a line
   of Agentforce code is written. Here's how we prevent that."
✅ "The 3 reasons healthcare AI projects stall — and how to avoid them"
❌ "We help enterprises achieve digital transformation" (no loss framing)

---

### 3. Status Quo Bias
The tendency to prefer the current state — "if it ain't broke..."

**Application:**
Enterprise buyers use status quo bias as a defense mechanism. The most
common outcome of a sales process is: no decision.

**How to overcome in Teqfocus copy:**
- Make the cost of inaction explicit and specific:
  "Every month without unified data costs your sales team [X] hours of
  manual reconciliation" [VERIFY/customize per client]
- Show that the current state is already changing (market pressure):
  "73% of healthcare leaders say AI solutions need better data integration
  to deliver results" [source: HIMSS survey — VERIFY]
- Use near-term trigger events:
  "If you've purchased Agentforce licenses, the data readiness clock has started"

---

### 4. Social Proof — Institutional Version
In enterprise, buyers trust institutions that look like them.

**Application:**
- Name the client's industry, size, and situation — not just the outcome
- "A 2,000-person healthcare payer" lands better than "a client"
- Summit Partner badge = institutional social proof (peer validation)
- 4.9 CSAT = quantified social proof (harder to dismiss than testimonials)

**Placement rules:**
- Social proof near CTAs — not buried at the bottom
- Industry-matched proof for industry-specific pages
- Specific over vague — "reduced prior auth cycle from 14 days to 2 days" [VERIFY]
  beats "improved operational efficiency"

---

### 5. Authority and Expertise Signals
People trust and follow credible experts.

**Teqfocus authority signals to deploy:**
- Salesforce Summit Partner (top tier — most buyers don't know what this means,
  so explain it: "fewer than X% of Salesforce partners reach Summit tier" [VERIFY])
- 400+ certifications — specific number beats "highly certified"
- Partner since 2012 — longevity signals stability and depth
- Named advisors: ex-Salesforce, ex-Deloitte — institutional credibility by association

**Copy application:**
Don't announce the credential. Surface it in context.
✅ "Our Data Cloud team — 40+ certified, built on 12 years of Salesforce delivery"
❌ "We are proud to be a Salesforce Summit Partner"

---

### 6. Reciprocity
Give genuine value before asking for anything.

**Teqfocus application:**
- White papers, assessments, and frameworks that solve a real problem
- "Data Readiness Assessment" as a lead magnet — gives actual value,
  positions Teqfocus as the expert, surfaces the problem Teqfocus solves
- TeqTalk episodes — pure knowledge transfer, no pitch
- Free tools or templates for common Salesforce/data challenges

**CTA implication:**
The more genuine value given before asking, the higher the conversion on
booking a call. Free assessments convert better than "talk to sales."

---

### 7. Anchoring
The first number seen influences all subsequent judgments.

**Application in Teqfocus pricing context:**
- Lead with the outcome value, not the engagement cost
  "Reducing prior auth cycle by 12 days saves a 500-bed hospital ~$X annually [VERIFY]
  — our engagement starts at $50K"
- When presenting tiers, put the largest package first
- When discussing Big SI alternatives, anchor their cost first:
  "An Accenture team ramps on your platform for 3–6 months before they start.
  We've been building on Salesforce and Snowflake since 2012."

---

### 8. Commitment and Consistency
Once people commit to a small step, they're more likely to take a larger one.

**Application:**
- Free data readiness assessment → paid implementation
- 30-minute discovery call → scoping workshop → full engagement
- TeqTalk episode → newsletter subscription → event attendance → deal
- Each micro-commitment increases the probability of the next one

**CTA design implication:**
Don't ask for the biggest commitment first. Design a commitment ladder.
✅ "Start with a 30-minute data readiness review — no obligation"
❌ "Contact us to begin your digital transformation"

---

### 9. Framing — The Same Fact, Different Response
How information is presented changes how it's received.

**Loss frame vs. gain frame — test both:**
- Gain: "Connect your Salesforce and data layers — AI that actually works"
- Loss: "Without unified data, your Agentforce investment won't deliver"

**Enterprise buyers generally respond better to loss frames for initial
awareness, gain frames for decision-stage content.**

**Certainty framing:**
- "Agile delivery — value from week 3, not month 6" > "rapid delivery"
- "Fixed-scope phase 1 with defined outcomes" > "flexible engagement model"
