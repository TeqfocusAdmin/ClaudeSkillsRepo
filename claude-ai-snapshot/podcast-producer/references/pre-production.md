# Pre-Production Reference — TeqTalk Podcast Producer

## What Pre-Production Covers
Everything that happens before the recording: understanding the guest, shaping the episode angle, and preparing questions that surface the real conversation — not the rehearsed one.

**Goal:** Walk into the recording knowing the guest well enough that you can push past their prepared answers into the insight underneath.

---

## 1. Guest Research Brief

Prepare one for every guest. The research brief serves two purposes: it informs the question guide, and it gives the host context to ask better follow-up questions in real time.

### Research Brief Structure

```
GUEST RESEARCH BRIEF — TeqTalk Episode [#]
Guest: [Full name]
Title: [Current title]
Organization: [Company / Institution]
Episode theme: [Working title / central angle]
Recording date: [Date]

---

BACKGROUND
[3–4 lines: career arc — what they've built, led, or transformed 
that's relevant to this episode. Focus on the last 5–7 years. 
No CV recitation — pick the 2–3 things that matter most 
for this episode's angle.]

KEY CREDIBILITY SIGNALS
→ [Specific role or decision that establishes their authority on this topic]
→ [Specific outcome, scale, or context — company size, patient volume, 
  transaction volume, etc. — that makes this concrete]
→ [Any public work — paper, talk, article — that reveals their POV]

THEIR KNOWN POV
[2–3 lines: based on public content (LinkedIn, interviews, articles), 
what does this guest already believe about the topic? 
What's their signature perspective?]

POTENTIAL TENSIONS TO EXPLORE
→ [Where their POV might push back against conventional wisdom]
→ [Where their industry experience might contradict the "AI hype" narrative]
→ [Where they've likely faced failure or unexpected friction — and learned from it]

INDUSTRY CONTEXT
[2–3 lines: what's happening in their industry right now that 
makes their perspective timely. What pressure are CIOs/CDOs 
in their sector navigating?]

TOPICS TO APPROACH WITH CARE
→ [Anything sensitive — recent company news, legal/regulatory matters, 
  personal career transitions to handle carefully]
→ [Competitive dynamics they may not be able to comment on]

SUGGESTED EPISODE ANGLE
[1–2 lines: the specific lens this episode should take — 
not just "AI in healthcare" but "why clinical AI fails 
at the data governance layer — from someone who's 
lived through both sides of that failure"]
```

## 2. Episode Topic Framework

Before writing questions, decide the episode's narrative architecture — the journey you want the listener to take.

### Framework Structure

**The Opening Problem**
What is the central tension or challenge this episode addresses? State it as a problem the listener already feels — not a topic to be covered.

> ✅ *"Most enterprise AI projects stall before they deliver value — not because the technology doesn't work, but because the data underneath it isn't ready."*
> ❌ *"Today we're discussing AI adoption in enterprise settings."*

**The Guest's Unique Angle**
What does this guest's specific experience add to the problem? What can they say that a generalist can't?

> *"[Guest name] has [built / transformed / survived] [specific thing] — which means their perspective on [problem] comes from actual decisions made under real constraints, not from advisory work."*

**The Journey Arc (3–4 Acts)**
Map the episode's narrative flow. Each act is a thematic chapter.

| Act | Theme | Key Question to Drive It |
|---|---|---|
| Act 1 | Current State — where they are and how they got there | "Take me back to the moment when [specific challenge] became real for you." |
| Act 2 | The Complication — what made it harder than expected | "What did you try first — and what didn't work?" |
| Act 3 | The Turn — what changed, what they figured out | "What was the insight that unlocked it?" |
| Act 4 | The Outlook — where this is heading | "What does this look like in 3 years — and what should leaders be doing now?" |

**The Episode's One Takeaway**
If a listener remembers one thing from this episode, what should it be? Write it as a sentence — not a topic.

> ✅ *"AI isn't a technology problem. It's a data governance problem. And most leadership teams are solving for the wrong thing."*
> ❌ *"AI and data are important for enterprise transformation."*

## 3. Pre-Interview Question Guide

### Structure: 20–25 questions across 5 zones

**Zone 1 — Origin Story (3–4 questions)**
Establish context. Where did they come from and how did they get to where they are?

- "Before we get into [topic], I want to understand your journey — what brought you to this role, and what's the work that shaped how you think about [topic] most?"
- "What's the problem you've been most obsessed with solving in your career — and why?"
- "Take me back to [specific moment / role / decision]. What was happening, and what were you trying to figure out?"

**Zone 2 — The Real Problem (4–5 questions)**
Surface the specific, honest version of the challenge — not the press release version.

- "What does [topic] actually look like inside a [industry type] organization — not the conference talk version, the real version?"
- "What's the part of [topic] that nobody talks about publicly but everyone in the industry knows is true?"
- "When [common approach to topic] fails — and it often does — where does it usually fall apart?"
- "What did you believe about [topic] 3 years ago that you've since changed your mind about?"
- "What's the hardest decision you've made on [topic] — and what made it hard?"

**Zone 3 — The Architecture / Approach (4–5 questions)**
Go deep on what they actually built or decided — specifics, not principles.

- "Walk me through what you actually built / implemented / changed — what was the architecture?"
- "What were the 2–3 decisions that mattered most? And what would have happened if you'd made different ones?"
- "What did you have to stop doing to make this work?"
- "Who on your team was most important to this — and what did they do that you couldn't have done without them?"
- "What would you do differently if you were starting today?"

**Zone 4 — The Lessons (4–5 questions)**
Extract the transferable insight — what the listener can actually do with this conversation.

- "What's the thing you wish someone had told you at the start of this?"
- "If a [CIO / CDO / RevOps leader] in [industry] is 6 months behind you on this journey — what's the one thing you'd tell them?"
- "Where do you see most organizations getting this wrong — and what does getting it right actually look like?"
- "What's the question the industry is asking about [topic] that's the wrong question?"
- "What's the question nobody's asking yet that they should be?"

**Zone 5 — The Outlook (3–4 questions)**
Give the listener a view of where this is heading.

- "Where does [topic] go from here — in your industry specifically?"
- "What's the thing that will be obvious in 3 years that most people can't see yet?"
- "What keeps you up at night about this — what's the risk that isn't getting enough attention?"
- "What are you personally working on or thinking about that you're most excited about?"

### Question Writing Rules
- **One question at a time** — never stack two questions in one
- **Open-ended always** — no yes/no questions in Zone 2–5
- **Specific over general** — "Tell me about the data governance decision you made in 2023" beats "Tell me about governance"
- **Give permission to be honest** — "Not the press release version, the real version"
- **Prepare 3–4 follow-up probes** — "Say more about that," "What made that harder than you expected?" "What did you learn from that?"
- **Never ask about Teqfocus or Salesforce specifically** — the guest is the expert, not a product endorser
