## 1. Short-Form Reel Scripts (45–90 seconds)

### Purpose
Reels are the highest-reach format in the TeqTalk repurposing stack. A great reel stops someone mid-scroll, delivers one sharp idea, and sends them to the full episode. A weak reel tries to summarize — and gets skipped.

**One insight per reel. Always.**

---

### Reel Structure

**Hook (0–5 sec) — THE MOST IMPORTANT PART**
Bold on-screen text + spoken claim simultaneously.
- Must be the sharpest, most debatable, or most surprising thing from the episode
- Must work as on-screen text AND as an audio hook at the same time
- Should create a pattern interrupt — assume the viewer is mid-scroll at 2x speed
- If the hook doesn't make someone stop, nothing else matters

`[DESIGN FLAG: large bold text overlay — keyword highlight animation on the key phrase]`

✅ *"Most AI projects fail before the model is even built."*
✅ *"Your Salesforce data is probably lying to you."*
❌ *"In today's episode, we discuss AI governance with [Guest Name]."*
❌ *"Welcome back to TeqTalk!"*

---

**Setup (5–20 sec)**
Brief context. Who is saying this and why their specific experience makes this worth listening to.
- 2–3 sentences max
- Name the guest's role + the specific context that gives them authority here
- Don't recite their resume — name the one thing that makes their view credible

`[DESIGN FLAG: guest name lower third — Name, Title, Organization]`

---

**The Insight (20–60 sec)**
The guest's key point — delivered directly, not narrated by a host voiceover.
- Write as if the guest is speaking (or use their actual transcript clip)
- One complete idea — no sub-points, no pivots
- Spoken language: contractions, short sentences, natural rhythm
- If corporate language creeps in, rewrite in plain English

---

**The Land (60–80 sec)**
One line that gives the viewer something to do with this insight.
- A reframe: *"Which means [implication]"*
- A challenge: *"The question is: [what they should ask themselves]"*
- An action: *"If you're navigating [situation], [what to do differently]"*

---

**CTA (80–90 sec)**
> *"Full episode on TeqTalk → link in bio"*

`[DESIGN FLAG: TeqTalk logo bug — lower right, persistent throughout]`
`[DESIGN FLAG: end card with episode title + link]`

### Reel Selection Guide: Picking the Right Moments

**Best moments for reels:**
- A claim that most people in the industry would disagree with or find surprising
- A short, complete story with a twist or unexpected outcome
- A specific number or data point that reframes the scale of the problem
- A moment where the guest contradicts conventional wisdom directly
- A concrete "here's what I actually did" moment — not a principle, an action

**Avoid for reels:**
- Long explanations that require setup to make sense
- Moments where the guest is uncertain or hedging excessively
- Anything where the context is so specific it won't land without the whole episode
- Guest introductions or bio moments — these are not insights

## 2. Quote Card Copy

### Purpose
Quote cards are static visuals — a guest quote on a branded background — distributed on LinkedIn and Instagram. They work as social proof and pull people toward the episode.

### Quote Selection Criteria
- **Standalone clarity** — the quote must make sense without any surrounding context
- **Brevity** — 3–4 lines max for design readability on mobile
- **Specificity** — the more specific, the better. Generic wisdom doesn't stop scrolls.
- **Attributability** — exact quote preferred. Close paraphrase only if the meaning is preserved perfectly.

### Quote Card Copy Format

```
QUOTE CARD [#] — TeqTalk Episode [#]

QUOTE:
"[Exact or close paraphrase — 3–4 lines max.
 If longer, cut to the sharpest part.]"

ATTRIBUTION:
[Guest Full Name]
[Title]
[Organization]

EPISODE TAG:
TeqTalk Episode [#]

CONTEXT LINE (optional — 1 line under attribution):
[Why this quote matters — only include if the quote 
needs a single line of framing to land properly]

---
DESIGN FLAGS:
[DESIGN FLAG: White #FFFFFF background. Quote text #59267E Poppins Bold. Guest name/title #7751E2. Accent bar or border strip #BD72EF. Logo bottom-right.]
[DESIGN FLAG: white or high-contrast text]
[DESIGN FLAG: Teqfocus logo — bottom right]
[DESIGN FLAG: TeqTalk episode badge — bottom left]
```

### Selecting 3–5 Quotes per Episode

Aim for variety across these types:
1. **The Thesis Quote** — the episode's central argument in one sentence
2. **The Surprising Claim** — something that challenges a common assumption
3. **The Specific Insight** — a concrete observation with a number, timeframe, or named context
4. **The Practical Advice** — something the reader can actually do
5. **The Future Outlook** — where the guest thinks this is heading

## 3. YouTube Animation Brief

### Purpose
The YouTube animation brief tells the video editor exactly what motion graphics to place on the full episode recording — what to highlight, when to show it, and how to style it.

**Animation philosophy:** Minimal, purposeful, conversational. Like WTF with Nikhil Kamath — overlays that help the viewer track the conversation, not perform on top of it.

### Animation Style Spec
- **Type:** Icon-based motion graphics + keyword text highlights
- **Placement:** Lower third and side panel — NOT full screen
- **When to animate:** Stats, defined terms, named frameworks, architecture concepts, guest name on intro and after cuts
- **When NOT to animate:** General conversation, storytelling moments, emotional moments
- **Motion:** Crisp — in and out in under 0.5 seconds for most elements
- **Font:** Clean sans-serif, Teqfocus brand typography
