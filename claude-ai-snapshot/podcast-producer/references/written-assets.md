# Written Assets Reference — TeqTalk Podcast Producer

## Assets Covered
- Episode Newsletter (email distribution)
- Show Notes (podcast platform)
- LinkedIn Posts (3–5 per episode)
- LinkedIn Article (1 per episode)

---

## 1. Episode Newsletter

### Format: 9-Section Structure (500–700 words total)

**Section 1: Title**
Episode number + guest name + central insight. Sharp and specific.

✅ *"TeqTalk #35: What a Public Healthcare CIO Learned About AI the Hard Way"*
✅ *"TeqTalk #32: Why Cancer Prediction Is Now a Data Problem — With Dr. Prasun Mishra"*
❌ *"TeqTalk Episode 35 — Healthcare and AI"*
❌ *"AI in Healthcare with Our Latest Guest"*

---

**Section 2: Episode Hook (2–3 lines)**
The sharpest or most counterintuitive thing the guest said. Not a summary — a pull that makes the reader want to listen.

Formula: [Surprising claim] + [Why it matters to this reader] + [Tension the episode resolves]

> *"Most healthcare AI projects don't fail because of the algorithm. They fail because nobody cleaned the data underneath it. Khaled Turk has seen this from both sides — as a CIO who's lived through failed implementations and as someone who's rebuilt systems that actually work."*

---

**Section 3: Guest Intro (3–4 lines)**
Who they are, what they've built or led, why their perspective is worth 45 minutes.

Include: current role + career highlight most relevant to this episode + the specific lens they bring (operator, researcher, CIO, technologist).

---

**Section 4: Value Bullets (3–4 bullets)**
What listeners will take away. Outcome-framed — not topic-listed.

✅ *"Why interoperability isn't a technical problem — it's a governance one"*
✅ *"The one metric that tells you if your AI rollout is actually working"*
❌ *"Discussion of healthcare data challenges"*
❌ *"Overview of Salesforce in healthcare"*

---

**Section 5: Thematic Takeaways (2–3 sections)**
Each section: bold subhead + 2–3 bullets + 1 pull quote from the guest.

```
**[Subhead — bold, sharp, outcome or tension-based]**
- [Bullet 1 — specific, not generic]
- [Bullet 2]
- [Bullet 3]

*"[Guest quote — exact or close paraphrase, in italics]"* — [Guest Name]
```

---

**Section 6: Use Case Spotlight (optional — include when a real implementation story exists)**
Format: Challenge → Approach → Outcome. If no specific implementation story exists, skip — do not fabricate.

---

**Section 7: Future Outlook (2–3 lines)**
Where does the guest think this is heading? What should the reader watch for in the next 12–24 months?

---

**Section 8: CTA with Episode Link**
> *"Hear the full conversation: [Episode Title] → [link]"*

If no link yet: *"[Episode link — insert before send]"*

---

**Section 9: Community Prompt**
One question to spark replies. Specific to the episode's central theme.

✅ *"What's the biggest data governance challenge you're navigating right now? Reply and let us know."*
❌ *"What did you think of this episode?"*

---

### Newsletter Anti-Patterns
- Don't open with "In this episode..." — lead with the insight
- Don't list every topic covered — choose one narrative thread
- Don't make Teqfocus the subject — the guest's insights are the story
- Don't write vague bullets — if it could apply to any podcast, rewrite it
- Don't skip the pull quote — a real guest voice makes the newsletter worth reading

## 2. Show Notes (Podcast Platform)

**Length:** 150–200 words
**Purpose:** SEO discoverability on podcast platforms (Spotify, Apple Podcasts) + context for new listeners
**Tone:** Third-person, concise, outcome-led

### Show Notes Structure

```
[Episode hook — 1–2 sentences. The sharpest thing from the episode.]

In this episode of TeqTalk, [Host name] speaks with [Guest Name], 
[Title] at [Organization], about [central topic — specific, not generic].

[Guest name] shares [2–3 specific things they discuss — outcome-framed]:
• [Specific insight or topic 1]
• [Specific insight or topic 2]
• [Specific insight or topic 3]

[1 sentence: guest credibility signal — what they've built or led]

Whether you're navigating [specific challenge relevant to this episode] 
or just beginning to think about [topic], this conversation gives you 
[specific thing they'll take away].

---
About TeqTalk: TeqTalk is Teqfocus' CXO-level podcast series 
featuring AI, Data, and Salesforce leaders across industries. 
New episodes every [cadence].

Connect with [Guest Name]: [LinkedIn URL]
Connect with Teqfocus: [Website / LinkedIn]
```

## 3. LinkedIn Posts (3–5 per Episode)

### Post Type 1: The Insight Pull (Core post — always produce this one)

**Structure:**
- Hook = sharpest or most counterintuitive thing the guest said
- Body (3–4 lines) = expand or react to the insight — add context, tension, or implication
- Attribution = *"— [Guest Name], [Title], TeqTalk Episode [#]"*
- CTA = link to full episode

**Rule:** The post must stand alone as an insight — the episode link is the bonus, not the point.

```
[Guest's sharpest claim — in their words or close paraphrase]

[2–3 lines: why this is harder / more interesting / more 
consequential than it first sounds. Add context the guest 
didn't have time to unpack.]

[1 line: the implication for [persona] — what they should 
think differently about after reading this]

— [Guest Name], [Title], TeqTalk Episode [#]

Full episode → [link]
```

## 4. LinkedIn Article

**Length:** 600–900 words
**Purpose:** Thought leadership + SEO + TeqTalk episode depth
**Approach:** Build a full article around the episode's central theme — not around the episode itself. The episode is supporting evidence, not the story.

### Article Structure

1. **Thumbnail title** — SEO-friendly, outcome-led, 8–12 words
2. **Challenge intro** (2–3 paragraphs) — open with the industry problem the episode addresses. Make the reader feel seen before you offer anything.
3. **Core insights** (3–5 subheads, 2–3 paragraphs each) — guest quotes as supporting evidence, not the main story. One insight per subhead.
4. **Teqfocus perspective** (1 paragraph) — how Teqfocus approaches this problem. Natural, not promotional.
5. **CTA** — *"Hear the full conversation: [Episode Title] → [link]"*

**Article subhead formula:**
- *"Why [Common Assumption] Is Wrong in Practice"*
- *"The [Architecture / Governance / Leadership] Decision That Actually Matters"*
- *"What [Persona] Leaders Are Getting Wrong About [Topic]"*
- *"The [X] Things [Guest Name] Did Differently"*
