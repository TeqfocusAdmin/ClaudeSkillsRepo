# Battlecard Reference — Teqfocus Sales Enablement

## What a Battlecard Is
A battlecard is a one-page, fast-access reference for sales reps facing a specific competitor or competitive situation in a deal. It answers three questions instantly: What are they likely saying? What are their real weaknesses? What do we say that wins?

Battlecards are internal — written for the rep, not the prospect.

---

## Battlecard Types

| Type | When to Use |
|---|---|
| **Competitor Battlecard** | Prospect is evaluating a named competitor alongside Teqfocus |
| **Situation Battlecard** | Prospect is considering staying with an internal team, delaying, or doing nothing |
| **Displacement Battlecard** | Prospect currently uses a competitor and Teqfocus is trying to win the replacement |

---

## Standard Battlecard Structure

### Header
- **Competitor / Situation Name**
- **Teqfocus Win Condition** — 1-line summary of how Teqfocus typically wins this matchup
- **Danger Signs** — 2–3 signals that this competitor/situation is in the deal (what the prospect says)

---

### Section 1: What They'll Say About Themselves (2–4 bullets)
How the competitor positions their strengths — as they would say it.
Write this fairly and accurately. Reps need to know what they're up against.

---

### Section 2: Their Real Weaknesses (3–5 bullets)
The gaps that matter — framed as buyer consequences, not trash talk.

**Format:** *"They [what they do or don't do] — which means [business consequence for the buyer]."*

Examples:
- "They stop at the CRM layer — which means the data feeding Salesforce is still fragmented and AI initiatives stall before they start."
- "They bring generalist consultants — which means you're paying premium rates for someone learning your industry on your dime."

---

### Section 3: Teqfocus Differentiators in This Matchup (3–4 bullets)
What Teqfocus does that directly counters their weaknesses. Specific — not generic brand claims.

**Format:** *"Teqfocus [specific thing we do] — which means [the outcome the buyer gets]."*

---

### Section 4: Landmine Questions (3–5 questions)
Questions the rep can ask in discovery that expose the competitor's weaknesses — without naming the competitor.

These questions work because they surface the problem the competitor can't solve.

Examples:
- "Who owns the connection between your data infrastructure and your CRM — is that one partner or are you coordinating multiple?"
- "When your last AI initiative stalled, where in the stack did it happen — the model, the data, or the application layer?"
- "How much of your current Salesforce configuration was built by your last SI vs. your internal team — and who's responsible for it now?"

---

### Section 5: Objections They'll Seed (2–3 objections + counter)
Common objections the competitor plants in the buyer's mind — and the Teqfocus response.

**Format:**
> **Objection:** *"[What the competitor says or implies about Teqfocus]"*
> **Counter:** *"[How to respond — factual, not defensive. One sentence if possible.]"*

---

### Section 6: Proof Point to Use (1–2)
The most relevant proof point or client story to use in this competitive situation.
- Industry-matched if possible
- Outcome-led — metric + timeframe
- Flag with `[ADD APPROVED METRIC]` if not yet verified

## Competitor Quick-Reference

### vs. Big 4 / Global SIs (Accenture, Deloitte, Infosys, Wipro, etc.)
- **They say:** Full-service, globally scaled, multi-industry capability
- **Real weakness:** Generalist teams, high ramp cost billed to client, limited Salesforce + data depth at the specialized level
- **Teqfocus wedge:** "We go deeper on Salesforce and data than generalist consultancies — and we don't bill you for ramping up on platforms we've built on for years."
- **Win condition:** When the buyer has been burned by ramp costs or surface-level Salesforce work from a big firm

### vs. Salesforce-Only SIs (Mid-tier Salesforce partners)
- **They say:** Deep Salesforce expertise, certified team, rapid delivery
- **Real weakness:** No data layer capability — leave the buyer coordinating a separate data vendor
- **Teqfocus wedge:** "We go beyond the CRM. If your data isn't ready, your Salesforce investment isn't either. We fix both."
- **Win condition:** When the buyer has a data modernization initiative running alongside Salesforce, or AI is on the roadmap

### vs. Pure-Play Data / Analytics Firms
- **They say:** Modern data architecture, cloud-native, AI/ML expertise
- **Real weakness:** No application layer — can't connect the data to Salesforce or operational apps
- **Teqfocus wedge:** "We don't stop at the pipeline. We connect data to the applications your business runs on — and build the AI layer in between."
- **Win condition:** When the buyer has already invested in Salesforce and needs data to feed it, or needs AI that surfaces in a CRM

### vs. Offshore / Low-Cost IT Vendors
- **They say:** Cost efficiency, large delivery bench, fast scaling
- **Real weakness:** No strategic depth, no Summit credentials, no industry IP — pure execution
- **Teqfocus wedge:** "We're a strategic partner with Summit credentials, industry IP, and accountability for outcomes — not hours."
- **Win condition:** When the buyer has been burned by poor quality or lack of ownership, or when the deal requires deep Salesforce expertise

### vs. "Do Nothing" / Internal Team
- **They say (internally):** We can figure this out ourselves; we'll add headcount
- **Real weakness:** Internal teams are stretched on BAU; no Summit-level expertise; slow to build vs. partner
- **Teqfocus wedge:** "Most internal Salesforce and data teams are stretched on BAU. We accelerate what they can't prioritize — and leave them with something they can maintain."
- **Win condition:** When internal team has been trying for 6+ months without progress, or when there's a hard deadline (Salesforce renewal, AI mandate, go-live commitment)
