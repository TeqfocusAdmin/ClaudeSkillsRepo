## Section 2: The Multi-Cloud Competitive Story

This is Teqfocus' core differentiator in sales conversations. Use it deliberately.

**The Problem With Competitors:**
Most enterprise technology problems are architecture problems — not platform problems. Data lives in silos. AI stalls because the foundation isn't ready. Salesforce underperforms because it isn't connected to where data actually lives.

**Single-Layer Partners fail here:**
- Salesforce-only SIs: Don't touch the data layer. Leave the client coordinating 3 vendors.
- Pure-play data firms: Don't connect to the application layer where teams actually work.
- Big 4 / Global SIs: Generalist coverage, premium billing rates, ramp-up at client expense.
- Offshore IT vendors: Delivery shops, not strategic partners. No Summit credentials, no industry IP.

**Teqfocus wins by owning the full chain:**
Data infrastructure (Snowflake, Databricks, cloud) → Intelligence layer (AI/ML, analytics) → Application layer (Salesforce, custom apps) — as one integrated partner with one throat to grab.

### Competitive Language That Works
| Against | Use This |
|---|---|
| Big 4 / Global SIs | "We go deeper on Salesforce and data — and we don't bill you for ramping up on platforms we've built on for years." |
| Salesforce-only SIs | "We go beyond the CRM. If your data isn't ready, your Salesforce investment isn't either. We fix both." |
| Pure-play data firms | "We don't stop at the pipeline. We connect data to the applications your business runs on — and build the AI layer in between." |
| Offshore IT vendors | "We're a strategic partner with Summit credentials, industry IP, and accountability for outcomes — not hours." |
| Internal teams | "We accelerate what your internal team can't prioritize — and leave them with something they can maintain." |

## Section 3: Buyer Personas — Sales Entry Points

### CIO / CTO (Enterprise)
- **Fear:** Another transformation that underdelivers; integration debt; AI without a foundation
- **Sales Entry Point:** Risk reduction + architecture credibility + Summit credential
- **Discovery Angle:** Where did your last major transformation stall? What does your current integration architecture look like?
- **Proof to Use:** Phased delivery, architecture stability, Summit credential as de-risker
- **Buying Trigger:** Failed project, AI board mandate, M&A integration, cloud migration stall
- **Deal Size:** Large — enterprise transformation, multi-phase
- **Champion Profile:** VP of IT, Enterprise Architect, or CTO reporting to CIO

### CDO / Head of Data & AI
- **Fear:** AI the business doesn't trust; data strategy without adoption; failed POC
- **Sales Entry Point:** Data maturity + AI readiness framework
- **Discovery Angle:** Where is your AI initiative today — and what's slowing it down?
- **Proof to Use:** Data Cloud + Databricks + Snowflake implementation outcomes
- **Buying Trigger:** Failed AI POC, Data Cloud mandate, CEO pressure on AI ROI
- **Deal Size:** Large to mid — data platform + AI layer
- **Champion Profile:** Data Engineering lead, AI/ML team lead, Analytics director

### RevOps / Ops Leader (Mid-Market)
- **Fear:** Salesforce nobody uses; customization that breaks; manual forecasting
- **Sales Entry Point:** Speed + practical CRM outcomes in 90 days
- **Discovery Angle:** What does your current Salesforce adoption look like? What's your team doing outside of Salesforce that should be inside it?
- **Proof to Use:** CRM adoption rate improvement, forecast accuracy, 90-day delivery
- **Buying Trigger:** Salesforce renewal, new VP of Sales, growth breaking process
- **Deal Size:** Mid — Salesforce optimization, AMS
- **Champion Profile:** RevOps manager, Sales Ops director, CRM admin lead

### VP of Sales / Revenue Leader
- **Fear:** Salesforce as an expensive CRM nobody uses; missed quarters; no pipeline visibility
- **Sales Entry Point:** Revenue impact + pipeline visibility
- **Discovery Angle:** How confident are you in your current forecast? What's the biggest thing slowing your sales team down?
- **Proof to Use:** Pipeline visibility improvement, sales cycle compression, Agentforce automation
- **Buying Trigger:** Missed quarters, manual forecasting, new sales motion (PLG, partner, expansion)
- **Deal Size:** Mid — Sales Cloud, Revenue Intelligence, Agentforce
- **Champion Profile:** VP Sales, Chief Revenue Officer

## Section 6: Sales Asset Voice Rules

### Always
- Every headline = a complete outcome or insight — never a category label
- Proof before pitch — credentials after you've established the problem
- Write for the economic buyer reading alone — every asset must stand without a presenter
- Frame Summit credential as risk reduction, not as a badge or achievement
- One clear next step per asset — not multiple options

### Never
- "Leverage," "synergy," "holistic," "best-in-class," "robust," "end-to-end solutions"
- Feature-listing platforms without connecting to outcomes
- Announcing Summit Partnership as news
- Fabricating metrics — use `[ADD APPROVED METRIC]` as a placeholder
- Generic CTAs like "contact us to learn more"

## Section 9: Competitive Battlecard Quick Reference

### Battlecard A — vs. Big SIs (Accenture, Infosys, Cognizant, TCS)

**How they position:** Scale, global delivery, "one-stop shop"
**Hidden weakness to expose:** Their Salesforce and data practices are separate — clients coordinate between them. Ramp-up time is billed to you. No Summit-level depth.
**Teqfocus counter-positioning:**
- "We bring Summit-level Salesforce depth that generalist firms can't match — and we've been doing this long enough that you're not paying for our learning curve."
- "We work across the data and application layer as one integrated team — they hand off between practices."
**Proof to use:** Specific Summit credential + case study showing faster time-to-value than expected
**Win condition:** When the client is tired of coordinating vendors and wants one accountable partner

### Battlecard B — vs. Salesforce-Only SIs

**How they position:** Salesforce specialists, deep CRM expertise, lower rates
**Hidden weakness to expose:** They stop at the CRM layer. AI initiatives, data-driven personalization, and modern analytics all require the data layer they don't build.
**Teqfocus counter-positioning:**
- "Salesforce is only as good as the data feeding it. A Salesforce-only partner leaves you with a CRM that underperforms because the foundation isn't there."
- "We implement Salesforce AND build the data infrastructure it needs — you don't need to coordinate a second vendor."
**Proof to use:** Case study where Teqfocus connected Salesforce to data layer and showed the difference
**Win condition:** When the client has already had a Salesforce implementation that underperformed

### Battlecard C — vs. Pure-Play Data Firms (Slalom, Sigma, boutique data)

**How they position:** Modern data stack expertise, AI/ML capability, engineering depth
**Hidden weakness to expose:** They build pipelines that don't connect to where decisions get made (Salesforce, ops systems). Data sits in the warehouse but doesn't reach the business.
**Teqfocus counter-positioning:**
- "We don't stop at the pipeline. We connect data to the applications your teams work in — which is where ROI actually lives."
- "A data platform without an application layer is infrastructure for its own sake. We build both."
**Proof to use:** Case study showing data + Salesforce + AI outcome — not just data architecture
**Win condition:** When the client's AI or analytics initiative is stalling because it's not connected to operations

## Section 8: Demo Script Framework

Structure every Teqfocus product or capability demo in 5 parts:

### Part 1: Anchor the Problem (2 minutes)
Before showing anything, restate their specific problem.
"Based on our discovery call, you're dealing with [X]. Is that still the right framing?"
Never start a demo without confirming the problem you're solving.

### Part 2: Set Up the Story (1 minute)
"What I'm going to show you is how [similar company in their industry]
solved [this exact problem] using [Teqfocus + platform]. By the end of this,
you'll see what [outcome] looks like in practice."

### Part 3: The Demo (15–25 minutes)
- Show the journey, not the features
- Narrate from the buyer's perspective: "As your CDO, you'd see..."
- Pause at each key moment: "What does this mean for your team?"
- Show the before/after — not just the after

### Part 4: Proof Moment (3–5 minutes)
"A [healthcare payer/FinServ firm/Hi-Tech company] we worked with
had this exact situation. Here's what changed after 90 days." [VERIFY timeline]

### Part 5: Next Step (2 minutes)
Single specific ask — never "any questions?" as the close.
"Based on what you've seen, the logical next step is [scoping session / data assessment / proposal].
Can we put 2 hours on the calendar for [specific date options]?"

## Section 9: Discovery Question Bank by Persona

Ask these before any demo or proposal. Not all at once — work them into conversation.

**For CIO:**
- "What's the one technology investment you can't afford to get wrong this year?"
- "When you think about your Salesforce and data infrastructure 18 months from now — what needs to be true that isn't today?"
- "What's your current thinking on the build vs buy vs partner decision for AI?"

**For CDO / Head of Data & AI:**
- "Where does your data pipeline currently break down before it reaches a Salesforce workflow?"
- "How many of your AI initiatives are in production vs still in proof-of-concept?"
- "What would have to be true about your data layer to make Agentforce actually work?"

**For RevOps / VP Sales:**
- "How much time does your team spend correcting Salesforce data that came in wrong?"
- "What's your current forecast accuracy — and how confident are you in it?"
- "If Salesforce showed your reps exactly what to do next with each account — what would change?"

## Section 10: Deal Qualification Framework (MEDDPIC for Teqfocus)

Before investing AE time in a deal, validate these criteria:

| Letter | Criterion | Qualifying question |
|---|---|---|
| **M** | Metrics | "What does success look like in numbers?" |
| **E** | Economic buyer | "Who signs the contract?" (Are we talking to them?) |
| **D** | Decision criteria | "How will you evaluate options?" |
| **D** | Decision process | "What does the path to a decision look like?" |
| **P** | Paper process | "Once decided, how long to contract?" |
| **I** | Identify pain | "What happens if this doesn't get solved this year?" |
| **C** | Champion | "Who inside is advocating for this to happen?" |

**Disqualify early if:**
- No economic buyer access after 3 touches
- No defined budget or timeline by second meeting
- No champion identified — "just exploring" with no internal driver
- Scope is below Teqfocus's minimum viable engagement
