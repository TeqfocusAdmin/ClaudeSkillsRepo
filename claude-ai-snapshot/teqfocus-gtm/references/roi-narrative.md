# Reference: ROI / Business Case Narrative

Use when a prospect needs to justify the investment internally.
Length: 1–3 pages or incorporated into proposal | Not a spreadsheet — a narrative

## Structure

### The Investment Context
- What the engagement costs (at level of detail approved to share)
- What it delivers — specific outputs and timeline
- What "success" looks like at 6 and 12 months

### The Cost of the Status Quo
Frame the cost of NOT acting — more powerful than ROI math alone:
- Operational cost: manual processes, rework, fragmented systems
- Revenue cost: missed pipeline, slow onboarding, poor retention
- Risk cost: compliance gaps, data quality issues, AI initiative failure
- Use client's own language where possible — reference discovery findings

### The Value Case (3 categories)
**Category 1: Efficiency / Cost Reduction**
- Example: "Eliminating manual data reconciliation across 3 systems saves
  [N] hours/week at [team size] — that's [FTE equivalent]" [VERIFY]

**Category 2: Revenue Acceleration**
- Example: "Reducing sales cycle by [X] days with automated CPQ and
  data-driven forecasting typically increases quota attainment by [Y]%" [VERIFY]

**Category 3: Risk Reduction**
- Example: "Unified data governance reduces audit prep time and
  lowers compliance risk across [regulatory framework]"

### The Payback Frame
- Don't calculate exact ROI — that's the client's job
- Frame the conversation: "Based on what we've seen in similar engagements..."
- Reference relevant case study outcome (same industry, similar scope)
- Suggest: "In our discovery session, we can help you map this to your specific numbers"

## Rules
- Never invent ROI numbers — always [VERIFY] or reference client-provided data
- Frame ROI as a conversation to have, not a number to claim
- The strongest business cases connect to what the buyer already cares about
- American English throughout
