# Events & Webinar Assets Reference — Teqfocus GTM Skill

## Event Types Teqfocus Runs
- Executive Dinners (CXO-level, 10–20 attendees, curated)
- Happy Hour / Networking (conference adjacent, e.g., Dreamforce)
- Lunch & Learn (mid-funnel, 15–40 attendees, educational)
- Webinars (virtual, thought leadership or product-focused)
- Roundtables (peer discussion format, facilitated)
- Conference Sponsorship Activations

---

## Invite Copy Structure

### For Executive / CXO Events (Dinner, Roundtable)
**Tone:** Exclusive. Curated. Peer-level. Not a sales pitch — a conversation.

**Structure:**
1. **Subject / Header:** Make them feel selected — not invited to a mass event
   - ✅ *"An evening with [X] CIOs tackling [specific topic]"*
   - ❌ *"You're invited to our dinner event"*
2. **The Why Now (1–2 lines):** What's happening in their world that makes this timely?
3. **The Room (1–2 lines):** Who else is attending — peer credibility is the draw
4. **The Conversation (2–3 bullets):** What they'll walk away having discussed — not "topics to be covered"
5. **The Details:** Date, location, time. Clean. No clutter.
6. **The RSVP:** Frictionless. One click or one reply.

---

### For Lunch & Learn / Webinar
**Tone:** Value-first. Educational. Not a product demo.

**Structure:**
1. **Headline:** Outcome the attendee will leave with — not the event name
   - ✅ *"How [Industry] Leaders Are Closing the AI Execution Gap in 2025"*
   - ❌ *"Teqfocus Webinar: AI and Salesforce"*
2. **The Problem (1–2 lines):** What challenge does this session address?
3. **What You'll Walk Away With (3 bullets):** Specific, practical, outcome-led
4. **The Speakers:** Name, title, credibility signal — one sentence each
5. **Details + Registration CTA**

---

### For Happy Hour / Conference Networking
**Tone:** Low-friction, social, peer-driven. Not a pitch event.

**Structure:**
1. **Header:** Location + vibe — make it feel like the place to be
2. **1–2 lines:** Who it's for + why this is better than another vendor booth cocktail hour
3. **Details:** Time, venue, capacity note if relevant ("space is limited")
4. **RSVP:** One click

## Landing Page Headlines (Webinar / Event)

Formula: **[What type of leader] + [are doing/discovering/solving] + [the specific outcome] + [context/timeframe]**

Examples:
- *"How Healthcare CIOs Are Solving the Interoperability Problem — Without a Full Platform Rebuild"*
- *"The Data Architecture Powering AI at Scale in Enterprise Financial Services"*
- *"Why Mid-Market RevOps Leaders Are Rethinking Their Salesforce Build in 2025"*

## Post-Event / Webinar Follow-Up Email

**Email 1 — Same Day (Within 2 Hours)**
- Subject: *"[First name], here's what we covered today"*
- Reference one specific moment from the session
- Attach or link the key resource (deck, recording, one-pager)
- CTA: One specific next step — not "let us know if you have questions"

**Email 2 — 3 Days Later**
- Subject: *"The question we didn't get to answer"*
- Reference a question asked during the session (or a common one in the industry)
- Provide the answer + connect to Teqfocus' relevant capability
- CTA: Book a follow-up conversation

**Email 3 — 7 Days Later (For Non-Responders)**
- Subject: *"One thing from [event name] you might find useful"*
- Share a piece of content (case study, tool, article) related to the event theme
- Soft CTA: "Worth a look — no follow-up needed if timing isn't right"

## Event Asset Writing Rules

- **Never lead with Teqfocus' name** in event copy — lead with the value to the attendee
- **Executive events:** The draw is the room, not the host
- **Webinars:** The draw is the insight, not the speaker's company
- **Always specify** what the attendee will *walk away with* — not what will be "discussed"
- **CTAs:** Calendar hold for exec events; registration form for webinars; RSVP link for networking
- For Dreamforce or major conference events: tie the copy to what's happening at the conference — make attendance feel like a natural extension of why they're already there
